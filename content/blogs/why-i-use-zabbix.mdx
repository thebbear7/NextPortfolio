Zabbix doesn't get much attention in the modern DevOps conversation — most of the energy goes toward Prometheus, Datadog, or cloud-native offerings. After running it in production at AlMuqeet Systems, I have a few thoughts on why it still earns its place.

## What Zabbix does well

**Agent-based monitoring with minimal configuration.** The Zabbix agent handles most common checks (CPU, memory, disk, network) out of the box. You don't need to write exporters or instrument your application to get basic host visibility.

**Templates.** Zabbix ships with templates for hundreds of services — databases, web servers, networking gear. You apply a template and the checks, thresholds, and graphs are configured for you. Getting from "installed" to "monitored" is fast.

**Alerting is built in.** Prometheus pushes alert routing to Alertmanager, which is a separate process to configure and maintain. Zabbix's action/trigger system handles it internally. For smaller teams, fewer moving parts is a win.

## Where it struggles

Zabbix's query language is nothing like PromQL. If you want to write custom aggregations or correlate metrics across services, it becomes cumbersome. Grafana datasource support exists but is limited.

The UI is also dated — functional, not beautiful. Teams used to Grafana dashboards will find it jarring.

## My take

For infrastructure monitoring on a budget (Zabbix is open source and runs fine on modest hardware), it's a solid choice. For application-level observability where you need flexible querying and custom instrumentation, Prometheus/Grafana is the better fit.

I use Zabbix for the "is this host alive and healthy" layer, and I'd reach for Prometheus when I need to understand what the application itself is doing.
